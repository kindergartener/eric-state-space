> In modern civilization particularly, no one can think fast enough to think
their own thoughts. If I’d been abandoned in the woods as an infant, raised by
wolves or silent robots, I would scarcely be recognizable as human. No one can
think fast enough to recapitulate the wisdom of a hunter-gatherer tribe in one
lifetime, starting from scratch. As for the wisdom of a literate civilization,
forget it.

Recently I stumbled upon Eliezer Yudkowsky's LessWrong post titled [Cached
Thoughts](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/2MD3NMLBPCqPfnfre/cached-thoughts). In
it, he touches on something I think about often: that most people harbor
ideas or beliefs based on past information. They provide a short path to
recalculating the answer to a question, giving us speed and alleviating mental
load, but are often stale and resist updating right under our noses.

First of all: wow! As if you couldn't find a better name for this phenomena. I
feel like this is one of those early-philosophical thoughts you have as a child,
a la "how do I know anyone else exists", yet most people rarely indulge in as an
adult. At least, I remember going through these motions before settling on the
explanation that different people have different thresholds for what constitutes
a recalculation, and then I cached that thought. So this blog post is me
evicting that thought and giving it air.

Throughout my life, I've met many different types of people. I've met those
who think critically about every action they take, and I've also met those who
don't, seemingly carried along by the currents of life, and most people fall
somewhere in the middle. And while people have different thresholds for what
constitutes an ideological cache eviction, those policies can be quite revealing.

As someone who loves problem solving, I'd like to think of myself leaning
towards the critical thinking side of the spectrum. But my problem is that these
activities, solving math and programming problems, are exactly the type of tasks that
call for creative problem solving. There's no creativity in using the
creativity. I still think these activities are a net-benefit as they train the
muscle of critical thinking, and I'm not immune to the modern mind-melting
pasttimes. I still doomscroll on Reddit and watch the occasional Instagram reel. But I'm not out here
rethinking how to brush my teeth or optimizing the distance between my steps, I'll keep those cached.
